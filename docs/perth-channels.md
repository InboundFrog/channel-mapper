## Where the numbers come from

The channel numbers are from the metropolitan tables in Wikipedia's *List
of digital television channels in Australia* (read October 2026), matched
to services by name. The IDs are from the export. Two adjustments:

1. **HD on the main number.** Wikipedia lists ABC's SD service on 2 with
   HD on 20, and SBS's SD service on 3. We swapped those, so ABCTV HD is on
   2, ABC TV (SD) on 20, SBS ONE HD on 3 and SBS ONE (SD) on 30. Typing the
   number from the guide should land on the best picture. Seven (7), Nine
   (9) and Ten (10) already had HD on the main number. A test checks that
   2, 3, 7, 9 and 10 are all HD services.
2. **SBS HD's number is inferred.** Wikipedia doesn't give a mainland
   number for SBS HD. It lists SBS on 3, and says Tasmania has them the
   other way round (HD on 3, SD on 30), which implies 30 for HD on the
   mainland. After the swap above, that number goes to the SD copy.

### Cross-check against Free TV's allocations

Free TV Australia's OP-41 sets the number ranges each network may use
([Issue 9, January 2024](https://www.freetv.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Free-TV-OP-41-LCN-Descriptor-Issue-9-January-2024.pdf),
Table 3). Every number in the Perth map falls inside its network's range:

| Network | One digit | Two digits | Three digits | Perth map uses    |
| ------- | --------- | ---------- | ------------ | ----------------- |
| ABC     | 2         | 20–29      | 200–299      | 2, 20–29, 200–204 |
| SBS     | 3         | 30–39      | 300–349      | 3, 30–36, 301–307 |
| Seven   | 7         | 70–79      | 750–799      | 7, 70–78          |
| Nine    | 9         | 90–99      | 950–999      | 9, 90–99          |
| Ten     | 1         | 10–19      | 100–149      | 1, 10–17          |

OP-41 gives Ten **1** as its single-digit number, which matches Wikipedia
putting Ten's SD service on 1 and `10 HD` on 10. OP-41 doesn't say which service goes
on which number inside a range, and says nothing about HD versus SD, so
those choices come from Wikipedia and the adjustments above. Within its
range, each broadcaster allocates numbers to its own services.

## Duplicates

Several numbers carry the same programme:

| Numbers   | Services                                                |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 7, 70, 71 | Three `7 Perth` services (sid 1376, 1377, 1380), all HD |
| 9, 90     | Two `9HD Perth` (1122, 1126)                            |
| 92, 95    | Two `9GemHD Perth` (1125, 1127)                         |
| 93, 99    | Two `9Go!HD Perth` (1123, 1128)                         |
| 10, 15    | Two `10 HD` (1665, 1668)                                |
| 20, 21    | Two `ABC TV` SD (737, 739)                              |

The names and service types don't say which copy is which, so the lowest
service ID gets the lower number. Because they're simulcasts, getting this
the wrong way round only changes which copy you land on, not what's
showing. On the TV, Info shows which copy is on screen.

## Other notes

- **Ten's SD service is on 1.** Wikipedia lists `10` (SD, MPEG-2) on 1, so
  typing 10 lands on the HD service.
- **`Channel 9 Perth` is Nine's SD copy, on 91.** Nine's main number, 9,
  is the HD service.
- **Radio.** ABC radio is on 25–29 and 200–204, SBS radio on 301–307. ABC
  PERTH (the local radio station) is on 25, which Wikipedia lists as ABC
  Local Radio.
- **Not broadcast here.** Wikipedia lists SBS WorldWatch on 35, but it's
  not in this export.
- **Datacasting.** `you.tv` (16), `gecko` (17), TVSN (77) and RACING.COM
  (78) are numbered, not hidden. Use `h` or `s` in the editor if you'd
  rather not land on them.

## Other cities

The maps for the other capitals (`maps/au/<state>/`) are untested. Their
service IDs come from Free TV Australia's DTTB Service Information
Register (Issue 16, June 2021), not from a real export. Each one's header
says what's missing. [city-maps.md](city-maps.md) has the details,
including how the register's Perth column compares with this export.
